Tue Minh Cao

PhD Student · University of Florida

Mechanistic Interpretability & AI Safety · Advisor: Prof. My T. Thai

My research develops methods to understand the internal mechanisms of large language models, with the goal of making AI systems more transparent, predictable, and safe. I focus on two complementary views of model internals: the activation space — via sparse autoencoders, circuit tracing, and feature geometry — and the weight space — via weight decomposition and weight-level steering. I believe that a unified understanding of activation and weight spaces is a necessary foundation for mechanistic alignment.

Current Projects

Interpretable Parameter Decomposition via Activation-Space Supervision

Project Lead

Adaptive Learning and Optimization Lab, University of Florida · Jul 2026 – Present

Using the activation space to decompose weight space into interpretable components, by jointly training parameter decomposition with a sparse autoencoder. Result: raised the interpretability Intruder score of parameter decomposition from 0.20 to 0.75 on a large model, over existing methods.

Misalignment Localization in Finetuned Weights

Project Lead

Adaptive Learning and Optimization Lab, University of Florida · Jul 2026 – Present

Locating the mechanism inside finetuned weights responsible for emergently misaligned behaviour, by aggregating gradients over behaviourally similar examples and decomposing them into sparse, interpretable atoms. Attributions are validated with circuit tracing and activation/attribution patching.

Self-Reported Misalignment Adapter

Project Lead

Adaptive Learning and Optimization Lab, University of Florida · Jul 2026 – Present

Auditing misalignment that lies outside the finetuning distribution, where behavioural testing on the finetuning data gives no signal. An automated auditing agent elicits out-of-distribution misaligned behaviour, and a model-agnostic LoRA adapter trained on it makes a finetuned model self-report unforeseen misalignment. Evaluated on cross-domain transfer — eliciting confession of medical-domain misalignment from a model finetuned only on misaligned code.

Weight-Space Source of Object Hallucination in Vision-Language Models

Project Lead

AI4LIFE, Hanoi University of Science and Technology · Sep 2025 – Present

Weight-space decomposition isolating the low-rank regions that encode the prior knowledge driving object hallucination, validated with activation and attribution patching. Result: established a negative result — prior-knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, Nullu) fail to localize this knowledge and induce language collapse, while finetuning-based mitigation introduces new mechanisms rather than removing the source.

ICLR 2025 2025

NeurFlow: Interpreting Neural Networks through Neuron Groups and Functional Interactions

Tue M. Cao, Nhat X. Hoang, Hieu H. Pham, Phi Le Nguyen, My T. Thai

International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2025

Replaces the neuron with the neuron group as the unit of circuit analysis. Result: patching a group of only 4 neurons removes a concept from the model, while patching individual neurons does not — and the method surfaced background-bias circuits in a vision model that existing approaches missed.

NeurIPS 2026 (Under Review) 2026

Semantic Optimal Transport for Sparse Autoencoder Feature Matching and Circuit Compression

Tue M. Cao, Nguyen Do, My T. Thai

Under review at Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026

Matches semantically equivalent SAE features across layers via optimal transport, enabling automatic supernode compression of feature circuits under proven stability and matching-optimality guarantees. Result: compressed open-source Neuronpedia circuits to 10% of their original size and improved matching performance by 20–30% over prior state of the art.

EMNLP 2026 (Under Review) 2026

Tree SAE: Learning Hierarchical Feature Structures in Sparse Autoencoders

Tue M. Cao, Hoang X. Nhat, Raed Alharbi, Phi Le Nguyen, My T. Thai

Under review at Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP), 2026

Shows that parent–child pairs in existing hierarchical SAEs do not actually share semantics, then introduces a reconstruction-conditioned tree SAE that learns genuinely hierarchical features. Result: hierarchical feature pairs over 40% more coherent than prior state of the art, at matched absorption and feature-splitting scores.

arXiv PDF
NeurIPS 2026 (Under Review) 2026

ReSS: Learning Reasoning Models for Tabular Data Prediction via Symbolic Scaffold

Chenlang Yi, Gang Li, Zizhan Xiong, Tue M. Cao, Yanmin Gong, My T. Thai, Tianbao Yang

Under review at Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026

Decision-tree-scaffolded reasoning traces for tabular prediction; I contributed the faithfulness and causal-sufficiency metrics. Result: up to 10% accuracy improvement on medical and financial benchmarks, with reasoning traces shown to be causally load-bearing rather than post-hoc rationalization.

IEEE T-ITS (Under Review) 2026

Sphinx: First Explain, Then Explore

Nguyen Do, Tue M. Cao, Tien Van Do, András Hajdu, Tamás Bérczes, My T. Thai

Under review at IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, 2026

Explains the failure modes of autonomous driving models — indecisiveness, incorrect visual grounding, and inconsistency between frames — then automatically generates critical scenarios from those failure modes to improve the model. Result: driving success rate above 80% across multiple models, more than 30% over prior state of the art.
