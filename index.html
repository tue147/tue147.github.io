Tue Minh Cao

PhD Student · University of Florida

Mechanistic Interpretability & AI Safety · Advisor: Prof. My T. Thai

My research develops methods to understand the internal mechanisms of large language models, with the goal of making AI systems more transparent, predictable, and safe. I focus on two complementary views of model internals: the activation space — via sparse autoencoders, circuit tracing, and feature geometry — and the weight space — via weight decomposition and weight-level steering. I believe that a unified understanding of activation and weight spaces is a necessary foundation for mechanistic alignment.

Current Projects

Semantic Optimal Transport for SAE Feature Matching & Circuit Compression

Under Review · NeurIPS 2026

Advisor: Prof. My T. Thai (University of Florida)

Matches semantically similar features distributed across all layers via optimal transport, enabling supernode clustering in circuit tracing (as in Anthropic's Neuropedia). Discovers modular feature groups sharing functional roles — including induction feature groups and bracket-detection groups — with theoretical guarantees on stability and optimality.

Tree SAE: Learning Hierarchical Feature Structures in Sparse Autoencoders

Under Review · EMNLP 2026

Advisors: Prof. My T. Thai (UF) · Assoc. Prof. Le P. Nguyen (HUST)

Identifies fundamental limitations in how existing SAEs define hierarchical features, then proposes the first tree-structured SAE that learns genuinely hierarchical feature pairs at multiple granularities. Demonstrates that the structure promotes hierarchical feature geometry in large language models.

Weight Decomposition for Hallucination Source Tracing in Vision-Language Models

Active

Advisor: Assoc. Prof. Le P. Nguyen (HUST) · Team Lead

Decomposes weight matrices to identify low-rank regions that encode prior knowledge driving object hallucination in VLMs. Shows that existing editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, Nullu) fail to localize prior knowledge and cause language collapse, while finetuning-based methods introduce new mechanisms rather than removing the hallucination source.

Systematic Benchmark for Activation Steering Methods

Active

Advisor: Assoc. Prof. Le P. Nguyen (HUST) · Team Lead

Constructs the first cross-evaluation benchmark for 30+ steering methods (weight steering, flow matching, representation finetuning, steering vectors, SAE-based steering), evaluated on alignment-relevant tasks including deception steering, refusal steering, and hallucination steering.

ICLR 2025 2025

NeurFlow: Interpreting Neural Networks through Neuron Groups and Functional Interactions

Tue M. Cao, Nhat X. Hoang, Hieu H. Pham, Phi Le Nguyen, My T. Thai

International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2025

Identifies polysemanticity and low per-neuron contribution as fundamental limitations of neuron-level interpretability. Proposes understanding model computation through functionally coupled neuron groups, traces group-level circuits to map how decisions propagate through the model, and evaluates on a range of tasks.

Semantic Optimal Transport for Sparse Autoencoder Feature Matching and Circuit Compression

Tue M. Cao, Nguyen Do, My T. Thai

Under review at Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026

Matches semantically similar SAE features distributed across all layers via optimal transport, enabling supernode clustering in circuit tracing (Anthropic's Neuropedia style). Tracks feature evolution across layers and identifies modular groups with shared functional roles (e.g., induction features, bracket detection). EMNLP 2026 (Under Review) 2026

Tree SAE: Learning Hierarchical Feature Structures in Sparse Autoencoders

Tue M. Cao, Nhat H. Xuan, Raed Alharbi, Le P. Nguyen, My T. Thai

Under review at Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP), 2026

Identifies fundamental limitations of existing hierarchical feature definitions in sparse autoencoders and proposes tree-structured SAEs that learn genuinely hierarchical features at multiple granularities. Advances understanding of hierarchical concept geometry in LLMs while maintaining performance on key benchmarks.

NeurIPS 2026 (Under Review) 2026

ReSS: Learning Reasoning Models for Tabular Data Prediction via Symbolic Scaffold

Chenlang Yi, Gang Li, Zizhan Xiong, Tue M. Cao, Yanmin Gong, My T. Thai, Tianbao Yang

Under review at Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026

Faithful and explainable reasoning for tabular data prediction guided by decision trees. Demonstrates that the generated reasoning traces are causally important, non-redundant, and less prone to hallucination. Designed and evaluated faithfulness and causal sufficiency metrics.
